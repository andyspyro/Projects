# Recurring Workforce Reporting and Audits

I maintained recurring HR reports that had to be accurate on a predictable schedule.

This included weekly workforce and vacancy reporting along with recurring data audits.

## Reporting Cycle

A typical reporting cycle involved:

1. obtain current source data
2. compare it with the prior reporting population
3. validate new, changed, and closed records
4. investigate discrepancies
5. refresh calculations
6. update the report
7. archive completed or historical items
8. publish the current view

## Standardization

I worked to make the reports consistent so the same metric meant the same thing from one cycle to the next.

This included standard definitions for:

* vacancy
* filled position
* active hiring process
* aging
* current stage
* closed item
* reporting period

## Monthly Audit Work

Recurring audits looked for:

* records that should have closed
* positions that changed status
* missing updates
* salary or step discrepancies
* unexpected differences across systems
* stale tracking records

## Why it mattered

A recurring report is a process, not a file.

The quality of the output depends on the repeatability of the validation steps that happen before publication.
